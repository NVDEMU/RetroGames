THE ATARI LEGACY

The Rise and Fall (and Rise again)

Founded in 1972 by Nolan Bushnell and Ted Dabney, Atari essentially created the arcade video game industry with the release of Pong. They later dominated the home console market with the Atari 2600 (originally the VCS) in 1977, bringing arcade experiences into the living room.

However, rapid growth led to a lack of quality control. The influx of poor-quality third-party games (infamously including E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial) contributed heavily to the Video Game Crash of 1983. Despite this, the brand survived, pivoting into home computers and attempting console comebacks throughout the 80s and 90s.

Atari 2600 (1977)

  • Code-named "Stella".
  • Sold over 30 million units worldwide.
  • Its joystick is arguably the most iconic video game controller in history.
  • Games were famously coded in 4KB of ROM or less!

Atari 5200 (1982)

  • Intended to compete with Intellivision.
  • Basically a repackaged Atari 400 computer.
  • Infamous for its non-centering analog joysticks which broke very easily.
  • Introduced a pause button on the controller!

Atari Lynx (1989)

  • The world's first handheld electronic game with a color LCD.
  • Developed by Epyx, bought by Atari.
  • Could be flipped upside down for left-handed players.
  • Required 6 AA batteries and drained them incredibly fast.

Atari Jaguar (1993)

  • Marketed heavily as the first "64-bit" console (Do the math!).
  • Actually utilized multiple processors, making it notoriously difficult to program for.
  • Alien vs Predator and Tempest 2000 remain its most highly regarded titles.
  • The controller featured a full 12-button numeric keypad.

Fun Fact

The word "Atari" comes from the Japanese board game Go. It is a term used when a stone or group of stones is in danger of being captured by the opponent, similar to "check" in chess.
